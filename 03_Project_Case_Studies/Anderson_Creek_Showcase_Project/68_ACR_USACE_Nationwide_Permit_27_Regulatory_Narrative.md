## II. Project Purpose & Ecological Restoration Objectives

The primary purpose of this project is to restore natural fluvial geomorphic function and ecological integrity to 1,500 linear feet of degraded pasture stream on Anderson Creek. The geomorphic design is specifically optimized to restore critical coldwater spawning habitats for wild **Brown Trout (`Salmo trutta`)** and **Brook Trout (`Salvelinus fontinalis`)**.

### Specific Environmental Objectives
1.  **Reduce Lateral Bank Siltation**: Regrade 3,000 linear feet of collapsing banks (both sides) from their vertical raw state back to a stable 3:1 bankfull slope. Geomechanically stabilize banks using double-net coir matting and high-density native plantings to reduce lateral sediment inputs by **98%** (280 tons/year reduction).
2.  **Restore Spawning Gravel Hydrology**: Constrict the overwidened channel from 24.2 feet to a stable 12.5 feet bankfull width. This constricts flow, locally concentrating bed shear stress during bankfull events to flush fine silts out of spawning runs, keeping the gravel substrate (D₅₀ = 22mm) clean and preventing egg suffocation.
3.  **Restore Summer Thermal Refuges**: Install **Rock Cross-Vanes** and **Log Step-Pools** that scour out deep, shaded thermal pools (4.5 feet depth). Plant native broadleaf trees across the 50-foot riparian buffer to establish high-density canopy cover, keeping summer maximum temperatures safely below the lethal **65°F** threshold.

## III. Proposed Geomorphic NCD Structure Specifications

To stabilize the channel bed and banks, we will install geomorphic structures utilizing native, non-acidic Blue Ridge Granite and rot-resistant hardwood logs (minimum 12" to 15" diameter) sourced directly from local timberlands:

### 1. Rock Cross-Vanes (Spawning Run Pools)
*   **Description**: An upstream-pointing "U" shaped rock structure that controls grade and centers streamflow down the thalweg.
*   **Material Specs**: 40 tons of angular Blue Ridge Granite Class III boulders (1.5 - 3.0 tons each). Top stones lock into the banks at a 20° angle, sloping down to a flat center weir.
*   **Fluvial Function**: Centers the stream's hydraulic energy down the middle of the channel, protecting the banks from lateral erosion. The flow passing over the center weir creates a plunge pool that scours out a deep sediment basin, providing critical summer holding pockets for adult brown trout.

### 2. Log J-Hook Vanes (Outer Meander Stabilization)
*   **Description**: A rock and wood structure placed on the outer curves of meander bends.
*   **Material Specs**: A 22-foot rot-resistant hemlock log vane (14" diameter) angled upstream into the bank at 15°, ending with an attached hardwood **Root Wad** positioned at the bank face. Boulders are placed as downstream footer stones.
*   **Fluvial Function**: Redirects flow away from outer banks toward the center of the channel. The root wad complex creates complex woody cover and provides micro-turbulent velocity pockets where juvenile trout can feed on macroinvertebrates while remaining shielded from main current forces.

### 3. Bioengineered Toe-Wood Structures
*   **Description**: A bank stabilization technique utilizing layers of interlaced native woody logs, branches, and root wads buried directly into the outer bank full elevations.
*   **Material Specs**: 15 hemlock logs with root wads, layered with live willow stakes and backfilled with sorted gravelly soil, wrapped in 700g/sqm double-net coir fiber matting.
*   **Fluvial Function**: Mimics natural undercut forest banks, providing excellent cover for large adult brown trout while geomechanically binding the soil bank matrix to prevent lateral migration.

## IV. Construction Sequencing & Turbidity Isolation Plan

To execute instream heavy excavation without releasing turbid water that would harm downstream trout populations, construction will be phased in **100-foot zones** working from upstream to downstream:

```
               PHASED 100-FT WORKSPACE SEDIMENT BYPASS
               
    [ UPSTREAM CLEAN WATER ] ──> [ temporary sandbag dam ]
                                            │
                                            ▼ (6" bypass diesel pump)
     ───────────────────────────────────► [ flexible HDPE pipe ] ──────────────────────────
    │                                       (routes flow around zone)                      │
    ▼                                                                                      ▼
  [ active excavator zone ] ──> [ sediment filter bag ] ──> [ turbidity curtain ] ──> [ CLEAN STREAM ]
    ( dry work channel )
```

1.  **Water Isolation Setup**: Install a temporary sandbag and clean-stone cofferdam upstream of the active 100-foot work zone. Place a high-capacity 6" diesel trash pump at the dam pool, routing clean streamflow around the active excavation zone through a heavy-duty flexible HDPE conduit, discharging it downstream onto a splash pad made of Class II rip-rap to prevent scour.
2.  **Instream Grading**: Grade outer banks using a 15-ton excavator equipped with biodegradable hydraulic fluid. Narrow the bankfull channel width, shape outer meanders, and excavate deep pools. Keep muddy work water isolated in the dry channel.
3.  **Active Work Water Filtration**: Pump muddy seepage water from the isolated work zone into a geotextile **Sediment Filter Bag** located in the adjacent upland buffer. This filters out all suspended clay and silt particles, discharging clean, clear filtrate water back into the stream below.
4.  **Structure Installation**: Install rock cross-vanes, J-hooks, and log step-pools. Stabilize graded bank slopes using double-net coir matting immediately, pinning the edges tightly using oak stakes.
5.  **Re-Watering Sequence**: Once the zone is fully stabilized, remove the sandbag dam slowly to allow streamflow back into the channel, capturing any initial turbid flushes behind a secondary floating turbidity curtain. Move the isolation setup downstream to the next 100-foot zone.
