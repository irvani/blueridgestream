## II. Project Narrative & Buffer Encroachment Justification

### Description of Proposed Activity
The applicant proposes to perform fluvial geomorphic stream restoration along 1,500 linear feet of degraded pasture channel on Anderson Creek. Instream operations require heavy excavation equipment (excavators, loaders) to enter and work within the state-mandated 25-foot buffer to grade vertical, collapsing clay banks to a stable 3:1 profile, install geomorphic rock/log structures, and plant a dense native forest canopy.

### Why Encroachment is Unavoidable
To stabilize the eroding pasture banks and restore a stable stream channel, heavy machinery must directly access the stream bed and bankful elevations. It is physically impossible to stabilize lateral bank failures, reconstruct natural meander curves, or place heavy boulder/log structures (rock cross-vanes, toe-wood complexes) without working directly within the 25-foot protective buffer.

### Net Environmental Benefit Narrative
Although this project causes temporary disturbance within the 25-foot buffer, it will result in a permanent, massive reduction in regional erosion. Active lateral pasture banks currently dump **286 tons of sediment per year** directly into the creek, choking the spawning gravels of wild **Brown Trout (`Salmo trutta`)**. Grading these banks back to a stable 3:1 slope, wrapping them immediately in double-net coir matting, and installing a high-density native forest buffer will reduce sediment loading by **98%**, permanently improving water quality.

## III. Fluvial Erosion & Sedimentation Control Plan

To ensure that land-disturbing activities within the 25-foot buffer do not release sediment downstream or violate Georgia's 25-NTU water quality standard, we will implement this robust **Fluvial Erosion & Sedimentation Control Plan**:

```
                  EPD SEDIMENT CONDUIT & FILTER DETAIL
                  
   [ cofferdam pool ] ────> [ 6" diesel trash pump ] ───> [ flexible HDPE conduit ]
                                                                   │
                                                                   ▼
   [ isolated wet work ] ──> [ 3" sludge pump ] ──> [ sediment filter bag ] ──> [ clean filtrate ]
```

### 1. High-Velocity Clean Water Pump-Around (GSWCC detail: Bypass-1)
*   **Dam & Pump Configuration**: At the upstream limit of each 100-foot construction zone, construct a temporary cofferdam using clean washed gravel covered in heavy-duty 10-mil plastic sheeting. Place a 6" diesel-driven high-performance trash pump at the dam pool.
*   **Bypass Conduit**: Route clean, unpolluted stream water through a flexible 6" HDPE pipeline around the active work zone, discharging it downstream onto a splash pad made of Class II rip-rap to prevent scour.
*   **Monitoring Standard**: The bypass system must operate continuously during active instream excavation, keeping the work area completely isolated from the active streamflow.

### 2. Work Area Seepage Silt Filtration (GSWCC detail: Sediment Bag-2)
*   **Seepage Ingress**: Active groundwater seepage and minor bank seepage will pool in the dry work channel.
*   **Filter Bag Siting**: Place a 3" high-sludge pump in the collection pool and route muddy water into a heavy-duty 15' x 15' non-woven geotextile **Sediment Filter Bag** placed on a flat, grassed upland area at least 30 feet outside the riparian buffer.
*   **Filtration Action**: The filter bag traps all suspended clay, silt, and sand particles. Clean, clear filtrate water flows out of the bag's pores and sheet-flows over grass back to the stream, ensuring zero sediment reaches the creek.

### 3. Double Sediment Barriers (GSWCC detail: Sd1-C)
*   Install two parallel lines of high-sensitivity Type C (tensile-strength reinforced) silt fencing along the entire landward boundary of the 25-foot work buffer, preventing any upland overland runoff from carrying silt into the stream.

## IV. Vegetative Buffer Stabilization & Planting Schedule

Upon completing bank grading, all disturbed areas within the 25-foot buffer must be stabilized geomechanically and vegetatively within **24 hours** using this dense planting matrix:

### 1. Geomechanical Bank Stabilization
Grade bank slopes back to a stable 3:1 slope, apply a native riparian seed mix (Switchgrass, Virginia Wildrye, Fox Sedge) at a rate of 25 lbs/acre, and cover immediately with **700g/sqm double-net Coir Fiber Matting (natural coconut husk matrix)**. Secure the coir matting tightly using 12" wooden stakes driven in a diamond pattern at 2-foot intervals.

### 2. Bioengineered Live Staking Schedule
Insert dormant, live vegetative cuttings through the coir matting directly into the bankfull elevations at a density of **1 live stake per 2 square feet**:
*   **Black Willow (`Salix nigra`)**: 900 live stakes (3' length, 1" diameter). Black willow roots rapidly, binding bank soils together.
*   **Silky Dogwood (`Cornus amomum`)**: 900 live stakes (3' length, 1" diameter). Provides excellent root network and bank structural stability.

### 3. Riparian Shading Tree Canopy Planting Schedule
Plant bare-root, high-status native trees within the 25-foot state buffer at a density of **400 trees/acre** to restore permanent coldwater shading for the brown trout spawning runs:

| Native Tree Species | Siting Placement Zone | Root Type | Shading Canopy Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eastern Hemlock (`Tsuga canadensis`)** | Bank bankfull toe | Deep coniferous | High-density evergreen year-round shading |
| **Tag Alder (`Alnus serrulata`)** | Instream bank water line | Dense fibrous | Nitrogen-fixing organic inputs for insects |
| **River Birch (`Betula nigra`)** | Mid-bank slope | Deep lateral | Fast-growing canopy, locks bank soils |
| **Rosebay Rhododendron (`R. maximum`)**| Buffer understory | Dense matting | High-density solar heat attenuation (LAI >= 5.8) |
