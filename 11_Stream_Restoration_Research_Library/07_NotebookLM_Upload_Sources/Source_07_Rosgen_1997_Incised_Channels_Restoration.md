* **Authors**: Rosgen, D.L.

---

## 1. Executive Scientific Abstract
This seminal conference paper establishes Dave Rosgen’s highly influential "Four Priorities of Restoration" for incised streams. It outlines the geomorphic mechanics, sediment transport dynamics, and hydrological benefits of raising channel beds to reconnect streams with their historic floodplains. This process-based design represents the foundational geomorphic logic of Natural Channel Design (NCD) for restoring deeply incised, degraded agricultural streams.

---

## 2. Experimental Methodology
* **Case Studies**: Monitored the geomorphic recovery, groundwater elevations, sediment yields, and flood attenuation capacity of multiple completed stream restoration projects across different regions of the United States.
* **Restoration Priority Classification**: Developed a standardized taxonomy ranking restoration strategies based on their geomorphic effectiveness:
  * **Priority 1**: Raise the channel bed to reconnect with the historical floodplain (creates new Type C or E channel).
  * **Priority 2**: Excavate a new floodplain at the existing stream bed elevation (creates stable channel within a wide trench).
  * **Priority 3**: Convert the stream into a new type within the existing incision (primarily widening the channel to stabilize banks, Type Ab or B).
  * **Priority 4**: Stabilize banks in place using civil armoring (rip-rap, concrete walls, or heavy geo-textiles) without geomorphic adjustment.

---

## 3. Core Scientific Findings
1. **Priority 1 Superiority**: Proved that **Priority 1** (floodplain reconnection) is the most geomorphically stable and ecologically rich approach. It completely eliminates bank erosion, drastically reduces sediment yield, and raises the groundwater table.
2. **Hydraulic Shear Stress Reduction**: Raising the stream bed enables high flows to easily spill over onto the wide meadow floodplain. This dramatically increases the cross-sectional flow area, reducing the hydraulic radius ($R$) and slope ($S$) during floods. As a result, boundary shear stress ($\tau = \gamma R S$) drops to near-zero, preventing the stream from downcutting or eroding downstream.
3. **Hydrogeological Restoration**: By plugging the old incised trench and raising the stream bed, the local water table rises across the entire valley flat. This creates off-channel stream-wetland mosaics (vernal pools) and boosts summer pasture soil moisture.
