# 🗺️ Fluvial Geomorphology: Rosgen Priority 1 Floodplain Reconnections

## I. Rosgen Natural Channel Design (NCD) Methodology
Developed by hydrologist Dave Rosgen, the **Natural Channel Design (NCD)** approach utilizes fluvial geomorphology principles to restore degraded, incised streams back to stable, self-regulating dimensions. 

By measuring "reference reaches" (stable streams in similar valley types), geologists and civil engineers design the ideal channel width, depth, sinuosity, and slope to ensure the stream can effectively transport its sediment load without aggrading (silt loading) or degrading (downcutting).

## II. The Four Priorities of Geomorphic Stream Restoration

When a stream has undergone severe incision (downcutting into a deep trench due to erosion or historical channelization), the geomorphic practitioner must choose from **Four Restoration Priorities** based on physical constraints and regulatory credit targets:

```
                      [THE FOUR RESTORATION PRIORITIES]
                      
   PRIORITY 1: Reconnect to Historic Floodplain   PRIORITY 2: Construct New Lower Floodplain
            Plug old channel, raise bed.                    Excavate wide lower bench.
            
         Historic Floodplain                             Historic Floodplain
      ___________        ___________                  ___________             ___________
                 \  New /                                        \           /
                  \____/ <-- Raised Bed                           \_________/ <-- New Channel
                 |======| <-- Earth Plug                          |         |
                 |      | <-- Old Channel                         |_________| <-- Lower Floodplain
                 \______/                                                         Excavated
                 
   PRIORITY 3: Excavate Narrow Floodplain Bench   PRIORITY 4: Hard Armor in Place (Rip-Rap)
            Confined within existing trench.                No geomorphic or ecological lift.
            
         Historic Floodplain                             Historic Floodplain
      ___________             ___________             ___________             ___________
                 \           /                                   \           /
                  \___ ___  /                                     \~~~~~~~~~/ <-- Rip-Rap /
                   \_|___|_/ <-- Channel Bench                     \_______/      Hard Armoring
```

### 1. Priority 1: Full Floodplain Reconnection (The Gold Standard)
* **Approach**: Reconstructs a completely new, stable meandering channel at the elevation of the **historic, upper floodplain valley**. The old, deep, incised trench is filled or plugged with earth.
* **Ecological Lift**: **Maximum**. Elevates the local water table, restores historic wetland pockets, and maximizes floodplain hydrology during minor storm events (spilling over the bank $1-2 \text{ times}$ annually).
* **Savannah District SOP Credit Yield**: **High** (typically yields up to $8.6 \text{ credits/LF}$ due to maximum ecological lift values).

### 2. Priority 2: Construct a New Active Floodplain at a Lower Elevation
* **Approach**: Reconstructs a stable channel at a lower elevation, but **excavates a brand new, wide active floodplain bench** within the valley.
* **Ecological Lift**: **Moderate-High**. Highly effective when steep valley slopes or adjacent property boundaries prevent raising the water table to the historic level. Requires substantial excavation and earthwork.

### 3. Priority 3: Excavate a Floodplain Bench within the Existing Trench
* **Approach**: Excavates a narrow, active floodplain bench strictly **inside the narrow, existing incised channel trench**.
* **Ecological Lift**: **Moderate-Low**. Mostly acts as an energy dissipator to prevent further downcutting, but fails to restore wide-scale wetland or groundwater processes.

### 4. Priority 4: Stabilize and Hard-Armor in Place
* **Approach**: Leaves the channel in its degraded, deep, incised state and armors the banks with concrete, gabions, or traditional rip-rap.
* **Ecological Lift**: **Zero**. Banned or highly discouraged under USACE Nationwide Permit 27 guidelines as it fails to restore fluvial processes and destroys local trout habitat.

## III. Mechanics of Priority 1 Valley Restoration

Executing a Priority 1 restoration is a high-reward geomorphic design that requires absolute precision:
1. **Thalweg Surveying**: Use RTK GNSS and drone photogrammetry to map the historical valley contours.
2. **Channel Re-routing**: Dig the new meandering channel on the historical floodplain surface, lining pool bends with root-wads and step-pools.
3. **Old-Channel Plugging**: Build a series of compacted earth "plugs" inside the old incised channel trench. The areas between the plugs are left open and allowed to fill with water, instantly converting the old degraded trench into a **rich, off-channel stream-wetland mosaic (vernal pools)**.
4. **Water Table Elevation**: Raising the stream bed instantly **raises the local groundwater table** across the entire valley. Upland pastures dry up less during summer months, and local timber stands receive sustained, stable sub-irrigation.

## IV. Nationwide & Regional Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Muddy Creek Priority 1 Restoration (McDowell County, North Carolina)
* **Context**: Muddy Creek, in the Blue Ridge foothills of North Carolina, suffered from severe historical agricultural channelization. The creek was forced into a straight ditch, causing downcutting up to 10 feet and destroying trout spawning gravels.
* **Intervention**: North Carolina environmental agencies and Trout Unlimited executed a massive Priority 1 restoration:
  * Restored **over 3 miles** of channel.
  * Plugged the old straight ditch, routing the stream into a newly designed, highly sinuous C4 gravel-bed channel across the historical meadow.
* **Result**: Raised the water table by **8 feet**, restoring 50 acres of historic wetlands. Post-project monitoring documented a **90% reduction in sediment loading** and immediate spawning usage by local trout.

### Case Study 2: Upper Truckee River Geomorphic Restoration (Lake Tahoe, California)
* **Context**: Historical cattle grazing in the high-alpine meadows of the Sierra Nevada had deeply incised the Upper Truckee River, lowering the meadow water table, drying out native vegetation, and sending tons of fine clay sediment directly into Lake Tahoe (threatening its celebrated water clarity).
* **Intervention**: Implemented a large-scale Priority 1 fluvial restoration:
  * Deactivated 1.5 miles of the deep, straight channel.
  * Constructed a new, shallow meandering channel that spills over onto the meadow floodplain during every spring snowmelt.
* **Result**: Successfully reconnected the river to **over 120 acres of meadow floodplain**. The meadow now acts as a natural sand filter, trapping sediment before it reaches Lake Tahoe, and raising groundwater levels to restore native willow and sedge thickets.

### Case Study 3: Soque River Valley Restoration (Clarkesville, Habersham County, Georgia)
* **Context**: A legendary private trout fishery in Northeast Georgia suffered from pasture widening, warm summer temperatures, and silt-loading, reducing wild trout holding capacity.
* **Intervention**: Hunter Morris's advisory network executed a geomorphic restoration:
  * Raised the stream bed, narrowing the bankfull width from a degraded, shallow 45 feet to a stable 25 feet.
  * Installed high-density woody bioengineering structures and log vanes to accelerate central currents, scour deep pools, and flush agricultural sediment to expose clean trout spawning gravels.
* **Result**: Restored premium holding pools and wild trout thermal refuge zones, multiplying wild trout densities and generating highly-valuable, high-end private spawning showcases.
