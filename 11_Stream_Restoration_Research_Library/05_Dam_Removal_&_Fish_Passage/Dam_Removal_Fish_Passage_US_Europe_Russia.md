# 🌊 Longitudinal Connectivity: Dam Removal & Fish Passages (US, Europe, Russia)

## I. Longitudinal Connectivity: The River Arterial System
Fluvial systems do not operate purely as isolated, local reaches; they are dynamic, longitudinal migration corridors. Obsolete mill dams, perched road culverts, and irrigation barriers act as environmental blockages, segmenting the river system. 

These barriers:
1. **Block Spawning Routes**: Prevent migratory trout, salmon, and sturgeon from accessing their upstream natal coldwater gravel beds.
2. **Warm the Water**: Create wide, shallow stagnant impoundments upstream, elevating water temperatures past the lethal $68^\circ\text{F}$ threshold.
3. **Trap Fluvial Sediment**: Impede natural gravel transport, leaving downstream reaches starved of spawning gravels and choked with fine sand.

In contrast to traditional American mitigation bank markets, European river restoration is heavily driven by **binding environmental policies** and cross-border collaborative frameworks.

### 1. EU Nature Restoration Law
Adopted recently, this landmark binding legislation sets a regulatory target to restore at least **25,000 kilometers of rivers back to a free-flowing state by 2030**. This is achieved by systematically removing obsolete dams, weirs, lock systems, and perched culverts.

### 2. Dam Removal Europe (DRE) & Open Rivers Programme
* **The Drivers**: DRE is a coalition of environmental organizations (WWF, World Fish Migration Foundation, Rivers Trust) that tracks and facilitates removals. The Open Rivers Programme acts as a primary private foundation funding barrier removals across Europe.
* **Scale**: Over **300-500 barriers** are now systematically dismantled across European rivers (specifically in Spain, France, Sweden, and the Baltic states) annually.

## III. International Case Studies & Border Watersheds

### Case Study 1: Baltic Sea Basin Migratory Sea Trout Restorations (RETROUT Project)
* **Siting**: Cross-border Baltic Sea basin, involving rivers across Sweden, Finland, Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania.
* **The Goal**: Restore migratory sea trout (*Salmo trutta*) population connectivity.
* **Interventions**:
  * Dismantled dozens of obsolete concrete mill weirs.
  * Replaced perched road culverts with open-bottomed, baffled arch culverts that allow fish passage during high spring runs.
  * Dumped hundreds of tons of sorted gravel-cobble spawning beds upstream.
* **Result**: Restored longitudinal connectivity across **over 120 river reaches**. Post-project benthic electrofishing documented wild sea trout spawning migrations and juvenile trout survival **booming by over 200%** within three years.

### Case Study 2: Hiitolanjoki River Restoration (Finland-Russia Border)
* **Siting**: The Hiitolanjoki River flows from Finland across the border into Lake Ladoga in Russia. It is the most critical river system for the critically endangered Lake Ladoga landlocked salmon (*Salmo salar sebago*).

```
                      [HIIOLANJOKI DAM BREACHING LIFECYCLE]
                      
    1. HISTORIC DAM (1920-2020)   2. DEMOLITION (2021-2023)    3. RESTORED RUN (2024+)
    
       Ritakoski Hydropower          Breaching Obstacle           Natural Rock Ramp
         _______________               _  _  _  _  _  _             _______________
        |               |             | \  \  \  \  \  |           /               /
        |   Concrete    |   =======>  |  \   Excavation|  =======>/   Flowing     /
        |   Obstacle    |             |   \  \  \  \   |         /    Plunge     /
        |_______________|             |____\__\__\__\__|        /_____Pools_____/
        =================             =================         =================
        Salmon Migration              Dam Dismantled            Unlocked Spawning Run
        Permanently Blocked           Channel Regraded          Ladoga Salmon Return
```

* **The Goal**: Unlock the upstream Finnish reaches for spawning, which had been permanently blocked for over a century by three historic hydropower dams (Kangaskoski, Lahnasenkoski, and Ritakoski).
* **The Interventions**:
  * In a historic cross-border effort, conservation coalitions acquired the three private hydropower dams.
  * Between 2021 and 2023, the dams were systematically dismantled one by one (Kangaskoski in 2021, Lahnasenkoski in 2022, and Ritakoski in 2023).
  * Regraded the dam sites into natural, high-gradient rock ramps, creating spawning rapids and deep plunge pool resting zones.
* **Result**: **100% longitudinal connectivity restored**. In the autumn of 2024, post-demolition monitoring confirmed that landlocked Lake Ladoga salmon had returned, successfully ascending past the former dam sites into their historic upstream spawning channels for the first time in **102 years**.

### Case Study 3: Russian Siberian & Ural Basins Sturgeon Protection
* **Siting**: Ural River Basin (flowing through Russia and Kazakhstan into the Caspian Sea) and the Ob-Irtysh Basin in Siberia.
* **The Goal**: Protect and restore migratory routes for endangered Caspian and Siberian Sturgeon (*Acipenser baerii*).
* **Context**: While Western Europe focuses on dam removals, Russian sturgeon conservation relies heavily on **hydrological flow mitigation** and seasonal regulatory releases from massive Soviet-era hydro-reservoirs on the Ob, Irtysh, and Volga rivers to mimic natural spring floods (spawning triggers).
* **Restoration Techniques**: In smaller high-gradient tributaries in the Ural foothills and Altai regions, local angling coalitions and environmental institutes execute small-scale restorations. They place Large Woody Debris (LWD) configurations and construct natural boulder fish passes around low-head agricultural weirs to unlock spawning creeks.

## IV. Field Engineering: Techniques for Hunter's Crew

For regional headwater restorations in North Georgia, Hunter Morris's active 12-to-20+ field crew can execute these cost-effective longitudinal connectivity upgrades:

### 1. Perched Culvert Replacements
* **The Hazard**: Corrugated steel pipe culverts under private timber roads are often perched (suspended above the downstream water surface), creating a vertical barrier that trout cannot leap.
* **The Upgrade**: Excavate the old pipe. Install a wide, **open-bottom concrete arch culvert**. Line the natural stream bed running through the arch with cobble and gravel, ensuring an uninterrupted thalweg and stable flow velocities.

### 2. Rock Ramp Fishways
* **The Hazard**: Private landowners often possess low-head concrete weirs or old pond dams that block trout migrations.
* **The Upgrade**: Rather than expensive total dam removal, construct a **Rock Ramp Fishway** immediately downstream of the weir. Lay a series of large, interlocking boulders at a gentle $5\% - 10\%$ slope, forming a sequence of stepped, rock-scoured pools. This allows trout to navigate up and over the weir via a series of short, manageable leaps.
