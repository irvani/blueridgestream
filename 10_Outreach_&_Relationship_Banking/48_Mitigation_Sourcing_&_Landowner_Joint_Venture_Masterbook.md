## I. The Landowner Storytelling & Message Crafting Framework

To acquire high-equity geomorphic stream restoration projects at scale, Co-Founder Hunter Morris must move past dry, legalistic pitch decks. Private landowners do not buy 'regulatory mitigation credits' or 'fluvial bankfull shear strength equations.' Instead, they buy the restoration of their **family heritage, ecological stewardship, and high-value physical land improvements**.

### 1. The Core Sourcing Dilemma
Rural mountain landowners across Fannin, Gilmer, Union, and Towns counties are historically skeptical of outside developers, corporate builders, and government agencies. Standard developer outreach triggers three major fears:
1. **The Fear of CUVA Property Tax Violations**: Landowners fear that a stream restoration project will violate their O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7.4 Conservation Use Assessment (CUVA), triggering a massive 10-year retroactive property tax penalty.
2. **The Fear of Land Loss / Government Taking**: They worry that registering a perpetual conservation easement means handing over their private deed rights or allowing public fishing access to their coldwater trout runs.
3. **The Fear of Geomorphic Destabilization**: They fear that heavy excavators and grading foremen will tear up their pastures, muddy their trout pools, and leave their stream banks in a worse state of vertical erosion.

### 2. Crafting the Sourcing Story: 'From Trophy Guide to Ecosystem Restorer'
Hunter Morris must frame himself not as a corporate contractor, but as a local champion of coldwater ecosystems. Sourcing pitches should center on the following narrative arc:
- **The Catalyst**: 'For 15 years, I guided fly-fishing drift boat floats on these mountain streams. I watched our trout pools slowly fill with red Georgia clay. I saw the spawning gravels smothered by sand from failing vertical mud banks. I realized that guiding wasn't enough—we had to restore these channels to their natural geomorphic form.'
- **The Geomorphic Solution**: 'We don't dump rip-rap boulders or concrete in your creek. We use geomorphic bioengineering—using massive native cedar root-wads, gravel riffles, and dense Rhododendron max geogrid soil transplants to weave your stream bank back into the forest. We restore the spawning gravels and deep cooling runs so wild brook, brown, and rainbow trout can spawn naturally.'
- **The Zero-Cost Partnership**: 'Under our 70/30 Joint Venture model, we cover 100% of the engineering, licensing, USACE PCN permitting, and excavation costs. We monetize the restored buffer by selling mitigation credits to corporate developers and ESG planners. You pay zero upfront costs and receive 30% of all credit payouts, generating a powerful revenue stream from unbuildable, eroding creek beds.'
