## 1. Executive Summary

Compensatory stream mitigation operates in a highly regulated, dual-track federal and state permitting environment. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) Savannah District governs federal Clean Water Act (CWA) Section 404 compliance, while the Georgia Department of Natural Resources (DNR) Environmental Protection Division (EPD) regulates state erosion control and trout stream buffer variances.

This Outreach Plan provides Hunter Morris with a highly structured directory of actual regional decision-makers, step-by-step agency coordination protocols, and ready-to-use communication templates to fast-track our **Draft Prospectus**, **Prospectus**, **Mitigation Banking Instrument (MBI)**, and **EPD Stream Buffer Variances** for Anderson Creek (Roya's Cabin) and Goldmine Hollow (Hunter's Cabin).

## 3. Step-by-Step Agency Coordination Protocol

To secure USACE and EPD approvals without costly design delays, Hunter must execute the following structured 4-stage coordination protocol:

```
                  CHRONOLOGICAL REGULATORY PIPELINE
                  
  [ Stage 1: Pre-App ] ──► [ Stage 2: MBI Draft ] ──► [ Stage 3: State Buffer ] ──► [ Stage 4: Execution ]
   Informal Site Visit       Formal Public Notice        DNR EPD Variance App       CE Recorded & Release
    Hammonds & IRT             USACE Savannah            Michael Berry & Kent         15% Capitalization
```

### Stage 1: Pre-Application Site Visit (Months 1–3)
*   **Objective**: Get Justin Hammonds and IRT agency representatives (EPA, USFWS, GA DNR Wildlife Resources) out to **Roya's Cabin at Anderson Creek** *before* spending capital on detailed HEC-RAS hydraulic modeling.
*   **Action**: Submit a conceptual **Draft Prospectus** outlining geomorphic degradation (bank shear stress, lateral erosion) and proposed Natural Channel Design (NCD) cures (J-hooks, root wads).
*   **Regulatory Meeting Protocol**: Walk the channel with the IRT. Hunter should lead the dialogue (using the geomorphic stream diagnostics walk talk-tracks in **File 13**) to establish technical credibility, demonstrating a deep understanding of wild trout breeding habitats, benthic substrates, and hyporheic flows.

### Stage 2: Formal Prospectus & Public Notice (Months 4–6)
*   **Objective**: Initiate the official 30-day federal public comment period.
*   **Action**: Submit the comprehensive **Prospectus** to the USACE Piedmont Branch. 
*   **Coordination Point**: Proactively engage regional conservation non-governmental organizations (NGOs) like the **Chattahoochee Riverkeepers** or **Trout Unlimited**. Share our restoration design to secure their letters of support, which offsets potential public opposition and fast-tracks USACE evaluation.

### Stage 3: State EPD Buffer Variance Filing (Months 7–9)
*   **Objective**: Obtain state EPD approval to operate heavy machinery within the protected 50-foot trout buffer.
*   **Action**: Apply for a **State Stream Buffer Variance** under Georgia EPD Guidelines through **Brian Kent** and **Michael Berry**.
*   **Engineering Defense**: Demonstrate that our buffer intrusion is purely temporary and ecologically restorative (e.g., live-staking tag alders, planting silky dogwood, stabilizing active mudslides). Use a reference case variance (such as **EPD Buffer Variance BV-067-23-03**) to prove design compliance.

### Stage 4: Mitigation Banking Instrument (MBI) & Release (Months 10–12)
*   **Objective**: Execute the final regulatory contract and secure the initial **15% credit release (1,935 credits)**.
*   **Action**: Coordinate with **Justin Hammonds** to finalize the MBI. Record the perpetual Conservation Easement in Fannin/Gilmer county land deeds, and establish our **$220,000 Financial Assurance Performance Bond**.
*   **RIBITS Entry**: Once the MBI is signed, coordinate with USACE Mitigation Branch staff to upload our project into the **RIBITS (Regulatory In-lieu fee and Bank Information Tracking System)** database, making our credits visible to Southeast developers.

## 5. Standard Operating Procedure for IRT Site Audits

When hosting Justin Hammonds, Brian Kent, or other IRT representatives on-site at Roya's Cabin or Hunter's Cabin, Hunter must execute the following field checklist to maximize regulatory trust:

*   **[ ] Establish Safety & Accessibility**: Clear the pathways along the stream bank in advance. Ensure access points are safe, stable, and marked with geomorphic survey stakes showing bankfull indicators.
*   **[ ] Technical Site Folders**: Provide each agency official with a high-durability waterproof folder containing:
    *   The conceptual geomorphic design map.
    *   A summary page of the Savannah District SOP credit yield projection (~12,900 credits).
    *   Pre-restoration survey photos showing active erosion hazards.
*   **[ ] Fluvial Diagnostic Walk**: Lead the IRT along the degraded reach. Point out exact indicators of instability:
    *   *Riffle embeddedness* (showing sand siltation clogging wild trout spawning cobbles).
    *   *Outer bank sheer tension cracks* (proving lateral bank failure due to overwidening).
    *   *Groundwater table decoupling* (showing stream incisement disconnect from the floodplain).
*   **[ ] Emphasize NCD over Rip-Rap**: Articulate clearly why concrete and rip-rap are destructive to trout waters (thermal heating, velocity transfer) and show exactly where geomorphic root wads and live willow stakes will absorb kinetic energy, naturally scour deep pool refuges, and filter runoff.
