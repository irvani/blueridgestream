## 1. Competitor Operational Workflows & Drone Benchmarking

To build a significant competitive advantage in Georgia, Hunter Morris must understand how corporate mitigation conglomerates structure their environmental workflows and project scales compared to traditional boutique civil contractors.

### A. Operational Workflow Comparison

| Operational Phase | Traditional Grading Contractors | PE-Backed Corporate Conglomerates (RES, EIP, Wildlands) | Blue Ridge Stream Restoration (Hunter's Venture) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Stream Assessment** | Manual walking surveys with static measuring tapes and basic rod-readings. High margin of error in high-gradient reaches. | Expensive surveyor crews using terrestrial LiDAR scanners and Total Stations. High cost, slow mobilization (3-6 weeks). | **RTK-assisted GPS terrain tagging combined with UAS (Unmanned Aerial System) aerial photogrammetry. High speed (1-2 days), sub-centimeter accuracy.** |
| **2. Stream Photography** | Standard hand-held camera or mobile phone photography. Hard to document structural geometries or canopy coverage. | Periodic commercial flights or high-cost outsourced mapping services. Visual data remains locked in engineering silos. | **High-frequency DJI RTK flight paths to build consistent orthomosaics, georeferenced 3D point clouds, and high-density terrain meshes.** |
| **3. Remote Sensing** | None. Standard visual checks for bank erosion. | Satellite imagery (Landsat/Sentinel) analysis. Coarse resolution ($10\text{ m/pixel}$), highly limited under heavy canopy cover. | **Multispectral sensor drone mapping QGIS analysis. Generates $1.5\text{ cm/pixel}$ NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) arrays to prove vegetation growth.** |
| **4. Ideal Project Scale** | Small local bank repairs ($100$ to $500$ linear feet). General grading, no regulatory credit generation. | Massive watershed tracts ($>5,000$ linear feet or $>100$ acres of wetlands). High mobilization threshold, uninterested in smaller high-yield sites. | **Boutique high-yield reaches ($1,000$ to $3,000$ linear feet) of high-gradient trout meanders. Maximizes geomorphic credit yield factor ($8.6\text{ credits/LF}$) via Priority 1 restoration.** |

### B. Competitor Project Size & Scale Benchmarks
*   **The Conglomerate Scale (RES & EIP)**: These institutional funds target large-scale agricultural degradation sites yielding over $15,000$ credits. They rarely bid on projects with under $5,000$ linear feet because their overhead, mobilization costs, and corporate hierarchies require massive credit volumes to achieve their target internal rates of return (IRR).
*   **The Boutique Local Scale**: Traditional excavators operate on small residential or agricultural bank repairs. They use heavy rock rip-rap and concrete, which are environmentally destructive and do not qualify for USACE Savannah District stream mitigation credits.
*   **Blue Ridge's Sweet Spot ($1,000$ to $3,000$ LF)**: Hunter's ideal project scale fits precisely in this gap. By targeting high-gradient, coldwater trout watersheds (e.g. Anderson Creek, Goldmine Hollow) on private estates and mountain cabins, he accesses lands that conglomerates ignore. Because these sites utilize natural geomorphic meanders (Priority 1 Natural Channel Design), they achieve a premium credit yield factor ($8.6\text{ credits/LF}$), generating high financial returns with low overhead.

## 4. EOS "Level 10" Board Meeting Agenda

To maintain extreme focus and prevent meetings from devolving into unstructured storytelling, the Board of Advisors will follow the strict, time-blocked **Traction EOS "Level 10 Meeting" format** (90 minutes total):

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     EOS LEVEL 10 BOARD AGENDA (90 MIN)                       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| [10:00 - 10:05] | Segue & Good News (Personal & Professional Highlights)     |
| [10:05 - 10:15] | Scorecard Review (Review 5 Leading Operational Metrics)    |
| [10:15 - 10:25] | Rock Review (Status of 90-Day Critical Priorities)         |
| [10:25 - 10:30] | Headlines (Customer, Landowner & Employee News)            |
| [10:30 - 10:35] | To-Do List (Review of Action Items from Previous Session)  |
| [10:35 - 11:25] | IDS Session (Identify, Discuss, Solve Key Issues)          |
| [11:25 - 11:30] | Conclude & Meeting Rating (Target: 8+ out of 10 score)     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Agenda Breakdowns & Accountability Controls:
1.  **Segue & Good News (5 mins)**: Establishes a collaborative, positive environment. Each board member shares one win.
2.  **Scorecard Review (10 mins)**: Hunter reviews the 5 leading scorecard metrics. Any metric that is off-track is dropped straight down to the IDS list.
3.  **Rock Review (10 mins)**: Hunter reports "On Track" or "Off Track" for his three 90-day Rocks (strategic goals). No discussion is allowed here; off-track Rocks go to the IDS list.
4.  **Headlines (5 mins)**: Review landowner relations (e.g. Roya Irvani's Anderson Creek feedback) and USACE Savannah District communications.
5.  **To-Do List (5 mins)**: Rapid-fire check-off of 7-day action items. The board expects a $90\%$ on-time completion rate.
6.  **IDS Session (50 mins)**: The core of the meeting. The board prioritizes the compiled issues list, identifies the root cause of the top 3 issues, discusses solutions, and assigns concrete to-do items. *Never move to Issue #4 until Issue #1 is completely resolved.*
7.  **Conclude & Rating (5 mins)**: Recaps the newly generated To-Do list, confirms any external messages to stakeholders, and has each member rate the meeting on a scale of 1-10 (focusing on efficiency).
